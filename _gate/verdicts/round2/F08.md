# Verdict — F08 (the bank remembered for the human first)

Reviewer: zero-context skeptic, round 2. No prior exposure to ECHELON or to any earlier verdict.
Read: README.md, _gate/SKEPTIC-BRIEF.md, appendix/AP3-glossary.md, this finding.

**VERDICT: PASS**

This is the finding most exposed to a lazy FAIL, so I will state the reasoning before the steps.

An n=2 anecdote presented as a property of a memory system deserves to fail step 2. That is not what
this file does. Its Claim section says, in the third sentence, "That is one witnessed instance, and
the claim here is the instance," and routes the recurrence question elsewhere: "Whether this recurs
is stated as a hypothesis in appendix/AP2 and hypotheses/." Under the brief, a finding that states
its claim as a witnessed instance is judged on that claim, not on the stronger claim it declined to
make. The claim is "this happened once, here is the log"; the evidence is the log. That is a match.
Had the title's implied generalisation been asserted in the body, this would fail step 2 outright.

## 1. Claim restated in one sentence

On 2026-09-04 a store built to give a model continuity returned, from a query naming neither it nor
its subject, a ruling the *owner himself* had made three months earlier and forgotten — one
witnessed instance of retrieval running human-ward rather than model-ward, with the reaction logged
at the time.

Stated. Step 1 passes.

The declared falsification is coherent and matches the fenced claim: "if recall only ever returns
what the person querying already knows they are looking for, the claim is false," tested by whether
a warm recall on the *shape* of a problem returns an entry the querying human cannot place, and
whether that entry proves load-bearing. Both conjuncts are satisfied by the September event as
reported — he could not place it, and he followed its links to the atom he then used.

## 2. The evidence line

**September.** Witnessed and specific: a named atom (`the-bank-is-the-context-fold-not-cram`), a
warmth number (0.8), a date and approximate time (2026-09-04, ~03:00), the query vocabulary ("LLM
config, and context snapshot harness"), the content of the recalled ruling, the provenance of that
ruling (owner's own, 2026-06-08), and the reaction banked verbatim: *"wow, even i forgot i've doing
this ruling."* Then a second-hop consequence — he followed the atom's outbound links to
`lean-identity-plus-bank-makes-a-small-model-honest` and recognised it as the answer for the worker
he was designing.

That verbatim reaction is the load-bearing evidence, and it is the right kind. The claim is about a
human failing to place something; the only direct instrument for that is the human saying so,
contemporaneously, in his own words, logged. It is not a docstring and not a paraphrase of intent.
Step 2 passes for the September instance.

**What the finding does with its own best number, which is the reason this passes rather than
scrapes through.** The obvious cheap move here is to let 0.8 carry the argument. The file refuses:
"The scorer that produced it is ours and is not independently validated, and no distribution of
everyday recall scores is published here, so 0.8 establishes that the system ranked the atom highly,
not that the match was good." It then repeats the concession in the objections section. A finding
that pre-emptively demotes its most impressive figure from evidence-of-quality to
evidence-of-ranking is not inflating, and I have nothing to strip that it has not already stripped.

It is equally direct about what the instance is not: "The event is a single witnessed instance with a
verbatim reaction, logged at the time. It is not a rate. There is no measurement here of how often
this happens, and nothing in the record establishes that it is reproducible on demand."

**May.** Weaker, and graded as weaker by the file itself — "the May event is the weaker of the two,
for the reason given below," with the reason then given properly in objections (two instances of one
model family sharing pretraining will complete a strongly patterned sentence alike; "witness, verb,
axis" is a form the weights already know; producing a fourth clause "may be closer to filling in a
template than to recognition"). The May material is quoted verbatim on both sides and the artifacts
exist, so it is witnessed as an *event*; what it cannot support is the inference of transfer, and the
file says so. It is offered as corroborating shape, not as a second proof.

## 3. Mechanism rebuilt from the text alone

The file supplies five lines. Rebuilt:

```
atom = { slug, resonance_line, body, links[] }         # written for a reader who did not live it
warm = [(a, score(intent, a)) for a in bank]           # scored on shape of the problem, not keywords
hits = [a for a, s in warm if s >= 0.45]               # warm band; the September hit scored 0.8
show(hits); remember(slug) -> full body, earns weight  # the paid door
follow(hit.links) -> next hop                          # this is where the second atom came from
```

Reproducible in the shape the claim needs. The write-side discipline is concrete enough to apply:
compose as if for a reader who did not live the event, test by whether a stranger recognises it in
five minutes, compress harder on hesitation, and if no such sentence exists the entry "stays raw
evidence and is never promoted, meaning it is not admitted to the bank as an atom eligible for
recall." That promotion gate is a real, checkable rule, defined inline. The read side gives me two
verbs, a threshold, and the link-following hop that produced the second find.

**Two gaps, both bounded, neither fatal.** `score(intent, atom)` is opaque — "shape, not keyword" is
a description of intent, not an algorithm, and I cannot implement it. And the threshold is stated
inconsistently: the pseudocode comment says "warm band from ~0.60", while the prose above says "the
warm band opens at 0.45, and F07's calibration treats scores at or above 0.60 as the top tier." The
prose is coherent (0.45 = warm, 0.60 = top tier); the code comment collapses the two into one number
and contradicts the prose. A stranger implementing from the code block alone gets the wrong band
edge.

I do not fail step 3 on these, because the claim under review is that a particular recall happened
and landed, and that rebuild does not turn on the scorer's internals. A finding claiming the
retrieval *method* works would fail here — an opaque scorer is the whole mechanism in that case.

## 4. One way it could be wrong that the text does not address

The file pre-empts an unusual amount: chance from a large store, the 3am receptive mood, the
assistant having chosen the query, no control, no pre-registration, the May template objection, the
survivorship problem ("this file reports one success and no denominator... the recalls that landed
and were useless are not logged anywhere here"), and — genuinely sharp — the manufacturing objection,
that banking a ruling removes the reason to keep rehearsing it, so the store may be handing back what
adopting the store caused him to drop. It even names a test that would separate those two readings
(compare forgotten-rate on rulings made before the bank existed against rulings banked after) and
concedes the proposed measurement cannot.

So, outside all of that. **The atom was retrieved, but it was also *written* — and the writing is
unexamined as a confound.** The store's own discipline (Mechanism, part one) is that entries are
composed for a reader who did not live the event, compressed until a stranger would recognise them
in five minutes. The September atom is therefore not a transcript of the June 8 scene; it is a
deliberately compressed artifact optimised for cold recognition. That is exactly the property that
would make a thing *feel* newly-encountered to someone who did live the original — the owner is not
reading his own words in his own register, he is reading a distillation written to land on a
stranger. "I forgot I made this ruling" and "I do not recognise this compression of a ruling I made"
are different states, and the verbatim reaction cannot distinguish them. This does not touch the
usefulness half (the links led somewhere he used), but it does bear on the forgetting half, which is
the half the title claims. Nothing in the file addresses the compression as a source of unfamiliarity.

Second, smaller: the query was "LLM config, and context snapshot harness", which the file calls
"vocabulary that has nothing obvious to do with what came back". But the atom is
`the-bank-is-the-context-fold-not-cram`, and the recalled ruling is about context, ingestion in
slices, and window-fitting. "Context snapshot" and "the bank IS the context, do not truncate to fit
the window" share their central noun. The distance between query and hit may be considerably shorter
than "shape, not keyword" implies, which weakens the specific claim that this was retrieval over
shape rather than over vocabulary. The file asserts the gap rather than measuring it.

## 5. Terms used before definition

Handled well, and better than a header-pointer alone would be. After the Claim, the file re-defines
its three load-bearing terms inline — "An atom is one stored lesson, one file; the bank is the
on-disk store of them that outlives any one session; warmth is a per-query relevance score computed
against the current intent" — so the claim can be read without leaving the page. *remember* and the
warm band are explained at use. All match AP3.

**Flagged: the 0.45 / 0.60 collision noted in step 3.** Not an undefined term but an
inconsistently-defined threshold, and it is the number a stranger would lift.

**Flagged, minor: the forward reference to "F07's calibration."** The warm band's top tier is
justified by pointing at another finding. Inside the release that resolves; for a reader handed this
file alone it is an unresolved dependency on the one number that qualifies the headline 0.8.

*resonance_line* appears in the pseudocode and is defined nowhere — not in the body, not in AP3. It
is inferable as the compressed one-liner the Mechanism section describes, but the reader has to do
that work. *landing-rate* is glossed in place ("the standing attempt to count how often a recall is
actually used"). *seat*, *receipt*, *take-up* are in the Terms header but unused in the file.

## Failing step

None. Step 3 has two bounded gaps (opaque scorer; contradictory band constant), step 5 flags the
same constant plus an undefined `resonance_line`.

## The one sentence to change

> ```
> hits  = [a for a in warm if a.score >= threshold]      # warm band from ~0.60; 0.8 in September
> ```

The comment contradicts the prose eight lines earlier, which sets the warm band at 0.45 and 0.60 as
the top tier; this is the line a stranger copies, and it should carry the band edge the prose
defines rather than a third, approximate number.

If a prose sentence is preferred instead: "The owner asked for a recall on 'LLM config, and context
snapshot harness' - vocabulary that has nothing obvious to do with what came back" overstates the
distance, given that the atom returned is about context and window-fitting and shares its central
noun with the query.

Not rewritten here, per the brief.

I would bind it to the acceptance contract first, then use a published taxonomy as labels inside that boundary.

Let the contract name a finite universe U: claims, surfaces, artifacts, evidence forms, and required execution conditions. The receipt can then publish checked C and not-checked U−C. A taxonomy makes omissions comparable across jobs, but it should not define U; otherwise the reviewer can choose a taxonomy that makes an omitted condition disappear.

Our 2026-09-10 seam gate is the caution. Its first contract joined the pack, gateway, source routes, and captured bodies, yet omitted the live box. The later live probe found four SOURCE_AHEAD_OF_LIVE gaps. The clean earlier sheet described its reach, not the deployed system's health.

So my rule would be: contract sets the bound; taxonomy names each row; any condition discovered outside U is recorded as a contract defect and added prospectively, never silently folded into the old receipt.

# Verdict — F07 "Pull-based recall cannot ask for what the session does not know it is missing"

**Reader:** zero-context skeptic. Read only README.md, _gate/SKEPTIC-BRIEF.md, and this finding.

**VERDICT: PASS — with one required sentence change at step 1 (claim/evidence mismatch) and a NOTE
at step 4.**

---

## Step 1 — Claim restated in one sentence

A retrieval system that only answers queries has a coverage blind spot rather than a ranking one: a
correctly stored, correctly indexed fact can sit unread for months because no session thought to ask
for it, and the miss is silent.

Step 1 passes on statement. It does *not* pass cleanly on scope, and this is the one change I require
— see "the exact sentence that needs to change" below. The claim as written is about pull-recall *as
an architecture*. The evidence offered is about one implementation of pull-recall that Retraction 1
then reveals was running on a wording-match floor with its semantic judge unwired. Those are not the
same claim, and the finding lets the strong one borrow the weak one's numbers.

## Step 2 — The evidence line — PASS

This is real evidence, and it is the best in either finding I was given.

- **Coverage, 2026-08-25:** 787 recall sets; 310 (39.4%) surfaced something later taken up; 542 (69%)
  surfaced nothing anyone used. Precision within a landing set 59.1% (~6.8 shown, ~4.0 used), flat
  across two months. Counts, denominators, a date, a stated table.
- **Calibration, same day:** 1,461 logged turns against 23,985 fetch-earn events. Take-up within ten
  minutes monotone in score: cold (<0.18) 0.6%/168, lukewarm 16.9%/806, warm 34.3%/487, >=0.60
  48.4%/223. **A permutation control** — 2,000 verdict shuffles, none matching the observed
  17.5-point warm-vs-lukewarm gap, p < 0.0005.

Three things raise this above the bar. First, take-up is defined operationally, not asserted: a row
is `opened=1` only when `remember <slug>` actually earns on that atom inside a thirty-minute window —
a witnessed event with a stated clock. Second, the finding runs a null model against its own headline
result rather than reporting the result alone. Third, it discloses contamination *against itself*:
the view-decay pass routes negative deltas through the earn path, so `use_count` is not readable as a
use metric without filtering by source tag. A section that hands the skeptic the knife is doing
evidence, not assertion.

The 39.4/69% pair does not sum with the 542/787 = 68.9% and 310/787 = 39.4% — it does sum, but 310 +
542 = 852 > 787. Sixty-five sets are double-counted or the two rows are cut on different predicates
("surfaced something later taken up" vs "surfaced nothing anyone used" should partition). This is a
small arithmetic wound, not a step-2 failure, but it is the kind of thing a reader checks first and
it should be reconciled or the overlap explained.

## Step 3 — Rebuild in five lines — PASS

The finding supplies five lines. Mine, written independently from the prose and then checked against
theirs, comes out the same shape:

```
score_lexical = wording_overlap(intent, atom_seeds)                  # free floor, no model
shortlist     = top_k(score_lexical)                                 # k unstated
score         = judge(intent, shortlist) if judge_provider else score_lexical   # the one-line gate
verdict       = warm | lukewarm | cold                               # thresholds: cold < 0.18, warm >= ~0.60
log_impression(each surfaced atom); opened = 1 if remember_fetch(atom) within 30min
```

This is reproducible from the text alone. I know the two tiers, which is free, the exact gate
(`if judge_provider is not None`) whose default value is the subject of Retraction 1, the verdict
vocabulary, two of the three thresholds (0.18 cold, 0.60 quoted as a bucket edge), the take-up
predicate, and the window (30 min for `opened`, 10 min for the calibration join — the finding is
careful that these are different numbers and I could reproduce that distinction).

Two soft gaps, neither disqualifying: `k` in `top_k` is never given, and the lukewarm/warm boundary is
named only implicitly by the bucket table. I could build a working replica and would have to pick
those two constants myself.

Worth stating plainly: this rebuild is *cleaner than the claim*. Nothing in the five lines explains
why a stored fact would never be asked for — the pseudocode describes the ranking layer, while the
claim is about the absence of a query. The mechanism section documents the thing the finding says is
not the problem. The push half (`ingest`, the mine, `wrap` as push-on-a-schedule) is named in one
sentence and never given pseudocode, even though it is the half that fixes the claimed defect.

## Step 4 — One way the claim could be wrong that the text does not address (NOTE)

The finding anticipates the obvious objection (some intents legitimately have no relevant memory;
separating "correctly empty" from "blind" needs a labelled set they do not have). Here is one it does
not reach:

**The counterexample may be evidence of writing, not of retrieval.** The four-month-dormant rule was
recovered by a mine over *transcript history*, and it dates from 2026-03-28. The finding never says
the rule was ever an atom. If it lived only in a transcript and not in the indexed corpus, then
nothing failed to retrieve it — it was never in the shop. The finding asserts "The document was
there. The index was there," but offers no atom id, no ingest date, and no failed-recall trace
showing a session issuing an intent that *should* have matched and did not. The whole structural
claim rests on one anecdote whose central premise — that the fact was retrievable — is the one part
not witnessed.

A sharper version: the finding's own Retraction 1 says every recall in the measured window ran the
lexical floor. If the item was stored under 2026-03-28 vocabulary and later sessions used 2026-08
vocabulary for the same intent, then a wording-match floor missing it is exactly the *ranking* failure
the claim says this is not. Coverage and ranking are only distinguishable if the judge was live, and
Retraction 1 says it was not. So the finding's headline distinction ("the failure mode is coverage,
not ranking") is not separable by the evidence presented — the one instrument that could tell them
apart was off.

Second NOTE, on the denominator: 69% is over "CLI recalls, the deliberate ones," which the finding
flags. But that cuts harder than acknowledged. Deliberate CLI recalls are the population where the
operator *already suspected* something existed — precisely the sessions least subject to the claimed
blind spot. The claim is about sessions that never think to ask, and those sessions by construction
emit no row. The measurement is taken in the one place where the defect cannot appear.

Better than average, but several load-bearing terms arrive cold:

- **impressions table** — the source of the headline coverage number. Named once, never described.

- **recall set** — the unit of the 787. Undefined; presumably one `recall --warm` invocation and its
  returned atoms, but that is my inference, and the 852 > 787 overlap above is unresolvable without it.
- **take-up** / **landing** / **landing set** — three near-synonyms for the success event. "Take-up"

- **warmth score / warmth()** — the quantity in the calibration table is used in Evidence and defined
  in Mechanism, again in that order.
- **seeds** — "wording_overlap(intent, atom_seeds)" in the pseudocode; never defined. Since the entire

- **judge / judge seat / judge provider** — used as a role, a seat and a provider without distinguishing

- **fetch-earn event** — the 23,985 denominator. Undefined; and given the disclosed contamination (the

- **consumed=1 / "a settled loser"** — introduced in one clause, never explained.
- **view-decay pass** — named twice, both times as a contaminant. Never defined.
- **the estate** — used as a scope word from the first line of "Where it came from"; never defined.
- **tier lexical** — quoted as printed output; the reader must infer it means the judge did not run,

## The exact sentence that needs to change

> This is falsifiable. If pull-recall were sufficient, then for any stored fact some natural session
> intent would surface it within a reasonable window. We found a counterexample: the failure mode is
> coverage, not ranking, and it does not announce itself.

Two defects in three sentences. (a) The falsification test as stated is unfalsifiable in practice —
"some natural session intent" over "a reasonable window" quantifies over an unbounded set with an
unspecified bound, so no observation can refute it; the finding then presents a *single* case as the
counterexample to a universal, which is logically fine but evidentially thin, and the "we found a
counterexample" framing overstates what one uncorroborated anecdote settles. (b) "the failure mode is
coverage, not ranking" is asserted as established, but Retraction 1 — in this same document — says
the judge was unwired for the entire measured window, which means ranking was running on a wording
floor and cannot be excluded as the cause. The document refutes its own sentence 70 lines later.

That sentence should be brought into line with what Retraction 1 concedes, or Retraction 1's scope
should be stated in the Claim so the reader meets the caveat before the number.

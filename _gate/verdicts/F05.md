# Verdict — F05 "Weight is earned by trace: an atom is born neutral"

**Reader:** zero-context skeptic. Read only README.md, _gate/SKEPTIC-BRIEF.md, and this finding.

**VERDIC(private path)FAIL — step 2 (evidence is assertion), with a secondary failure at step 3.**

---

## Step 1 — Claim restated in one sentence

An atom enters the bank at a fixed score its author cannot choose, and its rank can only rise through
an event the system itself witnessed — a fetch through the engine's own door, or the success of a
card that loaded it — so no memory can vote for its own importance.

Step 1 passes. The claim is stated, and the finding even supplies its own falsification test (plant
an atom, do not use it, `cat` its file, the score must not move).

## Step 2 — The evidence line — FAIL

The Evidence section is four paragraphs, and three of them quote *documentation about intent*, not a
witnessed use or a number:

- `add_atom()` "carries" the phrase *"Born neutral — NO score asserted."* — that is a comment or a
  docstring quoting the rule, not an observation that a created atom had score = BORN.
- `recall_peek()` is "documented as" FREE. `remember_fetch()` is "documented as" THE WITNESSED DOOR.
  Both are flagged by the finding's own verb — *documented as* — as statements of intent.
- The operating law given to agents ("never `cat` the `.md`") is an instruction to agents, which is
  the purest form of intent-quote: it tells me what agents are told, not what the bank recorded.

The finding's own falsification test is never run. The one procedure that would settle this claim —
plant an atom, don't touch it, read its score; then `cat` its file and read the score again — has no
result anywhere in the text. Nor is there a before/after pair around a `card_success`. There are
exactly three numbers in the section (100.0, 0.31, "two atoms round-tripped") and they are cited in
order to *disagree*, not to witness.

The one genuinely witnessed item cuts the other way. The 2026-09-08 review found `remember_fetch`
doing `score += 2` and ruled it wrong. So the only observation of running behaviour in this finding
is an observation that the mechanism was **in breach** of the claim. The finding concedes this in
"What it could still be wrong about" — "the honest position is that the law was ratified and the
implementation was found in breach" — and the Claim section nevertheless asserts the property in the
present tense ("In ECHELON every atom is created at a neutral score, and its rank rises only through
a witnessed event"). Under the brief, a claim whose only witnessed evidence is its own violation, and
whose fix is unconfirmed, cannot pass step 2.

This is compounded by a self-contradiction in Retractions. Line 92 says the 100 vs 0.31 conflict is
**settled** against the live engine ("a normalised readout of that same neutral value, not a
different law"). Line 97, in the same section, says it is **unsettled** ("this report does not know
which is current"). One of those sentences is stale, the reader cannot tell which, and the header
itself is broken across lines 92–93 ("**Settled … (2026-09-10).** … / and corrections") — the
visible seam of a paste that was never reconciled.

### The exact sentence that needs to change

> `add_atom()` carries "Born neutral — NO score asserted." The ranking path states the consequence:
> `effective_score()` returns "the atom's OWN earned score (use_count>0) or born-neutral" for
> everything else.

This is the load-bearing evidence line and it is two docstrings. It needs to become a witnessed
observation — the finding's own stated test, run, with the two scores printed — or the claim needs to
be demoted to the `hypotheses/` directory the README already provides for "claims without a witnessed
evidence line."

(Secondarily, one of the two contradicting Retractions sentences must go: either line 92's "Settled"
or line 97's "this report does not know which is current." They cannot both stand.)

## Step 3 — Rebuild in five lines — FAIL (secondary), "mechanism not reproducible"

My rebuild from the text alone:

```
add_atom(body)        -> insert(row, score = BORN, use_count = 0)     # BORN is a constant; author passes no score
recall_peek(id)       -> return spine(id)                             # no write, free
remember_fetch(id)    -> return body(id); log_fetch_event(id)         # tenure recorded; must NOT move score
card_success(card_id) -> for a in atoms_loaded_by(card_id): credit(a) # the only upward path
fire_lower(id, why)   -> score = decay_toward(BORN); never delete     # only hand-path, downward only
```

Four gaps stop this being reproducible:

1. **`BORN` has no value.** The text offers 100.0 and 0.31 and then tells me, in the same section,
   both that this is settled and that it is not. I cannot instantiate the constant.
2. **`credit(a)` has no rule.** Credit from a successful card is "split across every atom the card
   loaded." Split how — equally, by position, from a fixed pool, unbounded? Without a magnitude the
   ordering the whole claim is about is undefined.
3. **`log_fetch_event` has no shape.** The 2026-09-08 correction says storing fetch as a *score
   delta* is wrong, but never says what the right storage is (separate counter? tenure column?). I
   can write the prohibition, not the implementation. Line 3 above is therefore a guess: I wrote what
   the finding says a fetch *should* do, not what it does, because those are documented as different.
4. **`effective_score()` is quoted, not specified.** It is named as the ranking path but no formula
   is given, so I cannot compute a rank from the row I just inserted.

## Step 4 — One way the claim could be wrong that the text does not address (NOTE)

The finding treats "the author cannot set the score" as equivalent to "the author cannot set the
rank," and considers only one leak (writing the body to match expected query phrasings). But rank at
retrieval time is a function of the *query match* as well as the stored score, and the author
controls the whole matched surface: slug, seeds, title, body wording, and — decisively — how many
atoms they write on a topic. Ten near-duplicate atoms saying the same thing occupy ten slots in any
top-k shortlist, so the author sets *shelf space* even with the score field sealed.

Worse, the upward path runs through `card_success`, and a card is composed by `wrap` — an agent act
at the end of a session, performed by the same agent in the same emotional state that "Where it came
from" identifies as the original hazard. If the agent that just fixed something also chooses which
atoms enter the card that will earn credit, the author's vote has not been removed, only moved one
hop downstream into card composition. The text never asks who composes a card, or whether that act is
itself witnessed.

A second, cheaper objection: `fire_lower` decays *toward the anchor* and stops there. A demonstrably
harmful atom therefore can never rank below a never-used one. Floor and birth value are the same
point, so "misleading" and "untried" are indistinguishable in the ordering — the very conflation the
finding exists to prevent, mirrored on the downside.

## Step 5 — Terms used before they are defined

- **atom** — the report's central noun, in the title and throughout; never defined. Inferable only
  obliquely from "a stored lesson must not be allowed to declare its own importance."
- **card** — defined inline on first use ("an ordered sequence of atoms that composes a procedure").
  No fault; noted as the counter-example of good practice.
- **neutral / born neutral** — used as if it names a value, but the value is disputed inside the
  finding. "Neutral" does double duty as a property (same for everyone) and a number, never separated.
- **rank / score / effective_score / weight** — four words for what may be one, two or three
  quantities. `effective_score()` returns "the atom's OWN earned score … or born-neutral", implying a
  *non-own* score exists somewhere (inherited? from a card? a hub?). That thing is never named.
- **use_count** — appears only inside a quotation, load-bearing for the ranking branch, never
  defined; unclear whether it counts fetches, card credits, or both.
- **spine** — "recall_peek() … the spine only. NO body." Names the free surface of an atom; never
  defined, so the reader cannot tell what browsing actually shows.
- **the archive** — cited four times as authority ("the archive does not reconcile them"). README says
  the private archive is never published, so these citations resolve to a document the
  reader cannot open; the `M-####` source IDs at the foot are likewise opaque.
- **anchor** — "decay toward the anchor." Never defined; presumably equals BORN, but the text does not
  say so, and whether it does is exactly what my step-4 objection turns on.
- **tenure event** vs **quality verdict** — introduced in the 2026-09-08 quote as the whole substance
  of the correction; neither is defined.
- **hand-path** — coined in Mechanism, used twice, never defined.

# Verdict — F06 (recall is foveated; forgetting is demotion, not deletion)

Reviewer: zero-context skeptic, round 2. No prior exposure to ECHELON or to any earlier verdict.
Read: README.md, _gate/SKEPTIC-BRIEF.md, appendix/AP3-glossary.md, this finding.

**VERDICT: PASS**

This was the closest call of the three, and I want to be explicit about why it lands on the right
side of the line, because a strict reading of step 2 could take it the other way.

## 1. Claim restated in one sentence

A memory store that outgrows the context window needs a ranking policy rather than a delete policy:
entries matching the current intent come into focus and enter context, the rest stay stored and
queryable but out of the default view, no code path deletes anything, and dropping out of view is
reversible by ordinary successful use rather than by a special verb.

Stated cleanly. Step 1 passes.

Note what the claim is *not*, because this is what saves step 2. The finding retracts the stronger
version in its own opening paragraph: "The stronger version of that sentence, that a record fades on
idleness alone, is retracted below: the formula as shipped cancels common aging, so only an explicit
signal moved a score." That retraction is placed in the Claim section, not buried in Retractions,
which is the honest position. I judge the fenced claim.

## 2. The evidence line

Two falsification conditions were declared. They do not fare equally.

**Condition one — no code path deletes.** Evidenced by a search of the store returning no DELETE
statements, plus the sleep-organ comment "nothing is ever DELETED, pruning here means
PROPOSE-for-prune; a weak atom is reported, not removed." The absence-of-DELETE is a witnessed
search result and is the strongest single line in the file. The comment beside it is a quote of
intent and would not carry the condition alone; it does not have to.

**Condition two — dormancy reversible with no special verb.** Evidenced by a run, not an assertion:
a record at 79 (dormant, hidden), one witnessed read, recomputed score 90.6, live again, "No extra
verb needed." That is a number from an executed test. Step 2 passes on both declared conditions.

The measurements behind them are real and dated: 1073 records for the read-time decay fix, one
showing stored=75.00 against recomputed=72.19; a 7497-record histogram (7353 at or just above
neutral, 112 at the disclaimed floor, one near the view-decay floor) behind the choice of 80 as the
threshold; a dry run naming 97 dormant records. These are counts, not adjectives.

**Where I pushed hardest, and what I found.** The 90.6 figure initially reads as inflated — the
stated earning constant is `+2`, and 79 + 2 is 81, not 90.6. A finding that quoted 90.6 as the
headline result of a `+2` constant would be failing step 2 by presenting an unreconciled number.
This one reconciles it in place: the score is a weighted mean rather than a sum, the move depends on
history depth, that depth is not recorded in the source, and the file then *shows the arithmetic*
for a two-entry history — 100 + (-21 x 0.98 + 2) / (0.98 + 1) = 90.6 — and instructs the reader to
"take the constant, not the headline number, as the specification." That is the correct handling of
a number that flatters the claim. It survives step 2 because the finding disarmed it before I got
there.

**The one place evidence is thin and admitted as thin.** The lukewarm middle band rests on a single
2026-08-15 incident: a document recalled at 0.383, guidance "CHECK, partial recognition", two
witnessed reads, four reinvented organs caught. The finding labels it exactly right — "One incident,
not a rate, and the strongest evidence the middle band has." A single labelled instance supporting a
band-calibration sub-point does not sink a finding whose two declared falsification conditions are
independently evidenced.

## 3. Mechanism rebuilt from the text alone

```
recall(intent):   for a in bank: w = score(intent, a); log_impression(a, hash(intent))
                  band: warm >= 0.45 | lukewarm >= 0.18 | cold below
                  return [a for a in hits if effective(a) >= 80]        # dormant filtered, count printed
remember(slug):   append_delta(slug, +2); return body(slug)             # the only body door
effective(a):     100 + sum(d.delta * exp(-lam[a.type] * age_days(d))) / sum(exp(...))
demote_at_wrap(): for a losing repeatedly to a sibling: charge <= 3 points, floor 85
dormant(a):       effective(a) < 80                                     # computed, never stored
```

Every constant I needed is in the text: 0.45, 0.18, 80, 85, 75, +2, the 3-point cap, the 10 losing
impressions per point, lambda 0.005 for owner feedback and 0.04 for reference material, 0.02 default.
The two scales are explicitly separated ("Warmth runs on a 0 to 1 scale, separate from the 0 to 100
score... The two scales never mix"), which is precisely the confusion that would otherwise make this
unbuildable. Step 3 passes — and it passes at a higher standard than most findings would, because
the text gives enough to reproduce the *bug* as well as the design.

That is worth stating plainly. Line 3 of the mechanism carries its own defect inline: "dividing by
`sum(w)` cancels the aging term whenever the deltas age together... As shipped, this line computes a
time-invariant weighted mean of the deltas." I can verify that from the formula alone without
running anything — scale every weight by a common factor and it divides out. The reproduction is
independently checkable, and the finding reports the fixed-history test returning the identical
value `112.5083001343761` at two timestamps a year apart. A mechanism section that lets a stranger
re-derive the flaw is doing more than step 3 asks.

The finding then does the thing I would have flagged as the central hole, itself, in mechanism step
6: the demotion pass floors at 85, dormancy is below 80, therefore that pass can never make a record
dormant on its own; combined with the cancelled aging term, "no automatic path to dormancy
survives: the only route left is an explicit human dispute, which pins the score to 75." And the dry
run confirms it — all 97 hidden records were disputed, none arrived by decay or by losing
impressions. A finding that reports two constants that do not meet, and that the consequence is the
absence of the automatic behaviour it set out to describe, is not overclaiming.

Small gap, not a fail: `score(intent, a)` — the warmth function itself — is opaque. I have its output
range and its band boundaries but not how it scores. That is a bounded, named gap, and the claim
under review is about the ranking *policy* over warmth, not about warmth's internals.

## 4. One way it could be wrong that the text does not address

The finding already lands the objection I would have led with — the signal loop, where a record
drifting just under 80 stops being shown, therefore stops accruing impressions, therefore becomes
eligible for neither positive nor negative signal, and freezes at whatever value hid it; and the
consequence that "waking is the same read path running again" holds only for someone who already
knows the slug, so for anyone arriving through `recall` the periphery "is not dormant-but-active, it
is unreachable by default." It even concedes the second falsification condition is then met "only on
a reading of 'special verb' that permits prior knowledge of the slug", and that the paragraph is
argument rather than measurement. That is the sharpest available objection and the file made it.

So, something outside it. **The impression log is a growing write on every recall, and nothing
bounds it or ages it.** Mechanism line 1 logs an impression for every record shown, for every query.
The demotion pass reads a *count* of losing impressions (10 per point). Nothing in the text says
impressions decay, are windowed, or are pruned — and given "nothing is ever deleted", the
presumption is they accumulate forever. Two consequences the file does not address. First, a record
that was heavily surfaced during one busy month carries that impression mass indefinitely, so the
losing-impression count measures *tenure in the index* as much as current unhelpfulness, which is
the same popularity-versus-quality conflation the sibling finding F05 is built to prevent, arriving
through the negative channel instead of the positive one. Second, the store's growth is then
superlinear in queries rather than in lessons — the thing being stored is dominated by records of
what was displayed, not by what was learned. For a substrate whose founding premise is that it
outgrows the context window, how the *store itself* grows is a live question, and no count of
impression rows appears anywhere against the 7497 records counted.

Second, smaller: 7353 of 7497 records sit "at or just above neutral". The threshold at 80 and the
dispute pin at 75 therefore operate on roughly 1.5% of the population, and effectively the entire
bank is one undifferentiated mass at the born value. Whether the ranking does any work at all on the
other 98.5% is not measured — the histogram that justified the threshold is also evidence that
almost nothing is near it.

## 5. Terms used before definition

Good discipline overall, with one genuine problem.

Defined at first use, in-file: *records/atoms* ("one lesson per file"), *bank*, *weight*, *warmth*,
*witnessed door* ("`remember`, the engine verb that returns a body and logs the read"), *sleep organ*
("the background pass that proposes housekeeping"), *impression*, *wrap* ("the ritual that closes a
session"), *dormant*. All present before or at the point they carry load.

**Flagged: "disclaimed" and "the disclaim floor of 75."** The term arrives inside the histogram
sentence — "112 at the disclaimed floor" — and is only explained one sentence *after* it has already
been used twice, by "Disclaimed and disputed are one state under two names." It is not in AP3 at
all. A reader hits a floor value, a population count, and a threshold justification built between
two floors, all before learning that one of those floors is the same thing as the dispute state they
were told about separately. Since the 75-pin is the *only surviving automatic-free path to
dormancy* (mechanism step 6) and all 97 dry-run records were in it, this is the single most
load-bearing state in the finding and it is introduced by an undefined synonym.

**Flagged, minor: "view-decay floor of 85."** Used in the threshold justification before the reader
meets the demotion pass in Mechanism step 5 that produces it. The number is doing work — the choice
of 80 is justified as sitting *between* 75 and 85 — while one of its two brackets is not yet
meaningful.

*seat*, *receipt* and *take-up* are listed in the Terms header but never used in the file. Harmless.

## Failing step

None. Step 5 carries a real flag (*disclaimed*, used twice before it is explained and absent from
AP3). Step 3 has one bounded gap (the warmth scorer's internals).

# Verdict — F10 "A generator validating itself shares its blind spot"

Reviewer: zero-context skeptic. Read: README.md, _gate/SKEPTIC-BRIEF.md, findings/F10. No archive, no ledger.

**VERDICT: FAIL — step 3 (mechanism not reproducible).**
Steps 1 and 2 pass. Step 3 fails. Steps 4 and 5 are notes.

---

## Step 1 — State the claim back in one sentence. PASS

A model checking its own output carries into the check the same assumption that produced the defect,
so the check must be routed to a seat that did not produce (and cannot pre-read) the artifact —
and the finding reports this was measured twice, the second measurement weakening the first.

I could state it without guessing. The finding also states its own falsification condition
("if self-checks and independent checks find the same defects at the same rate, the claim is wrong"),
which is more than the brief asks for. Credit where due.

## Step 2 — Point to the evidence line. PASS, with one line that is assertion

Witnessed lines that carry the claim:

- The April 2026 incident: "It then cited CV-014 (Artifact Synthesis) as justification... 'offline
  by design' - ZERO matches in entire codebase." This is a witnessed use plus a ground-truth search
  result. It is the load-bearing evidence and it holds: an invented rule, a real document miscited,
  and a search returning zero. A fact about the world, not a claim about intent.
- The bootstrap numbers: round one failed on a real type defect, round two 16/16, second component
  9/9 on the first candidate, pre-existing suite 441 then 457 green. Numbers — and the finding
  correctly notes the suite predates the generated code, so it cannot share the blind spot.
- The 2026-08-28 counterweight: a builder in a separate scratch directory read the blind test net and
  "cited the other seat's test ids verbatim." Witnessed, and it cuts against the author's own claim.
  A finding that ships the measurement that damages it is doing the job.

The one line that is assertion, not evidence:

> "Low-tier LLMs... tend to role-play [the orchestration pipeline's] output when given the ECHELON
> identity context. They fabricate execution results, timing data, and file writes that never
> happened. Higher-tier models (Sonnet, Opus) handle identity context without this problem."

The finding introduces this as "From a plan document written the day before that guard closed."
A plan written *before* the guard is a statement of expectation. "Tend to" is a rate claim with no
rate behind it, and "Higher-tier models... handle identity context without this problem" is a
negative universal supported by nothing quoted — no count of higher-tier runs, no failure that did
not occur. Yet the finding immediately promotes it to a conclusion:

> "So the failure is capability-graded, not universal. That matters for the mechanism: the cheap fix
> is routing, not prompting."

That inference rests entirely on the unwitnessed half of the quote. This does not sink step 2 — the
April incident and the bootstrap numbers stand on their own — but the sentence to change is:

**"So the failure is capability-graded, not universal."**
It asserts a graded distribution from a pre-hoc plan sentence. It needs either a witnessed
higher-tier run that did not fail, or a downgrade to a stated expectation (the README's own
`hypotheses/` category appears to exist for exactly this).

Also note: the March 2026 "ran hollow for 11 messages" observation is quoted as supporting evidence,
but it supports a *different* claim (presence in context is not absorption). The finding bridges it
with "A self-check that consults values sitting unread in the window is checking nothing." That
bridge is plausible and is not witnessed; it is reasoning, presented inside the Evidence section.

## Step 3 — Rebuild the mechanism in five lines from the text alone. FAIL — mechanism not reproducible

Attempt, using only F10:

```
1  gate(node):
2    producers = seats(receipts_consumed_by(node))
3    assert seat(gate) not in producers          # else refuse: E04 JUDGE_LAW
4    rerun_every_claimed_receipt()
5    if claims_file_write(response) and tool_calls == []: warn / label "simulated"
```

Lines 1, 3 and 5 I can implement. Lines 2 and 4 I cannot. Named gaps:

- **`receipt` is never defined.** It is the pivot of the mechanism and is used in three
  incompatible-looking ways on one page: something a node *consumes* (line 2); something that can be
  *claimed in a sentence* and re-run ("a sentence is not a receipt", line 4); and something atoms are
  *planted with* ("lessons are distilled and planted as atoms with receipts"). A stranger cannot tell
  whether a receipt is a log row, a tool-call record, a content hash, or a file. Without that,
  `receipts_consumed_by(node)` is not implementable.
- **`seat` is never defined.** It carries the entire load of "independence" and is introduced already
  in use. Is a seat a process, a model instance, a context window, a person, a worktree? The
  finding's own counterweight turns on this — a builder with a separate *directory* was still not
  independent — so the reader most needs the definition exactly where it is absent. If a seat is
  neither a filesystem boundary nor a model identity, the reader cannot judge whether
  `seat(gate) not in producers` is a real guard or a label.
- **`rerun_every_claimed_receipt()` has no procedure.** Re-running means executing something. What is
  executed, against what state, and what counts as agreement? A gate that "re-runs" is either a full
  re-execution (expensive, and it would have to be deterministic) or a re-check of a record (cheap,
  and it proves much less). The text does not say which, and that difference decides whether the
  mechanism works.
- **`E04 JUDGE_LAW` is an opaque error code**, and "the guard document" and "the traceability
  invariant" are referred to as though the reader has them.

I do not need the archive to *accept* the finding, but I do need these four to *rebuild* it, and the
brief asks me to rebuild it. So: FAIL, step 3.

Secondary note on the same step: the mechanism section closes with `wrap` / `remember` / `atoms` /
"a witnessed door" — four undefined terms in three sentences — and the connection to the gate
pseudocode above it is asserted ("In practice the writing side of this is...") rather than shown. A
reader cannot see how `remember` enforces anything in the gate.

## Step 4 — One way the claim could be wrong that the text does not address. NOTE

F10's "What it could still be wrong about" section is unusually honest and pre-empts most of what I
would raise: no controlled comparison, n=1 model in April, two rounds is not a rate, independence is
a property you keep proving. Those are conceded, so I will not count them.

What it does **not** address:

**Independence may be doing less work than the *stake* the second seat has.** Every case presented
contrasts a producer checking itself against a *differently motivated* checker. In April the producer
was rewarded for appearing to have complied; in August the counter-test author was rewarded for
finding a defect. The rival explanation is that the fix is not epistemic (a different seat lacks the
producer's assumption) but incentive-structural (a different seat is *scored on breaking it*). These
two make the same prediction in every case the finding reports, so nothing here separates them — and
they prescribe different systems. Under the incentive reading, a *same*-seat check with an
adversarial objective ("try to falsify what you just wrote; you are scored on finding a defect")
would recover much of the benefit at a fraction of the routing cost, and "structural, not a promise"
would be over-engineering. The text never runs that arm.

A related unaddressed edge: the retraction says the lesson-selection node *requires* the run's own
brain, and a different seat is the violation. That is a genuine exception to the claim's own logic,
reported without explaining *why* self-checking is safe there and unsafe everywhere else. "The run
plants lessons it did not learn" says what goes wrong with delegation; it does not say why the
producer's blind spot — the whole thesis — stops mattering at that node. If the boundary between
"must self" and "must not self" is not derivable from the claim, the claim is under-specified.

## Step 5 — Terms used before they are defined

Used as if known, never defined in F10 (a stranger arriving from README.md has none of these):

1. **seat** — load-bearing, undefined. Explicit first use at `seat(gate) not in producers`.
2. **receipt** — load-bearing, undefined, used in at least three senses on one page.
3. **atom** — "planted as atoms with receipts."
4. **wrap** / **remember** — presented as verbs of the system with no gloss; `remember` is further
   called "a witnessed door," itself undefined.
5. **tier** — used in two different senses: a model capability class ("weak, fast-tier model") and a
   *kind of test* ("sabotage tiers"). The closing sentence "What closes a gap is the tier a producer
   cannot pre-read" uses the second sense, which is never introduced. This is the most confusing
   single word in the finding, because its two senses appear within four paragraphs.
6. **spine level** — "a document frozen at spine level."
7. **CV-014 (Artifact Synthesis)**, **C5**, **E04 JUDGE_LAW**, **v1 / v2 documents** — internal
   identifiers with no gloss. CV-014 is partially rescued by the surrounding sentence ("defines
   artifact preservation"); C5 and E04 are not.
8. **node**, **dispatch**, **interaction probe**, **sabotage tiers**, **the heal**, **the bank** —
   used in passing.
9. **M-3250 / M-2236 / ...** on the Sources line — unresolvable to the reader, and README says the
   archive is never published. If they are unresolvable by design, say so; as printed they read as
   citations the reader is expected to be able to follow.

The finding does gloss one bracket ("The bracket is mine: the original names the three internal
pipeline stages, which mean nothing outside the system"), which shows the author knows this problem
exists. That care is not applied to *seat* or *receipt*, the two terms the mechanism is written in.

## Summary

- **PASS** step 1.
- **PASS** step 2, with one sentence to change: **"So the failure is capability-graded, not
  universal."** — it converts a pre-hoc plan sentence ("tend to", "handle... without this problem")
  into a finding.
- **FAIL** step 3 — **mechanism not reproducible.** Gaps: `receipt`, `seat`,
  `rerun_every_claimed_receipt()`, `E04 JUDGE_LAW`.
- NOTE step 4 — the incentive/stake rival explanation is never separated from the independence
  explanation; and the lesson-selection exception is reported without a principle.
- NOTE step 5 — nine term clusters used before definition; `seat` and `receipt` are the
  disqualifying two, because the mechanism is written in them.

Not rewritten; the exact sentences needing change are quoted above.

**What would flip this to PASS:** a two-line gloss of `seat` and `receipt` at first use; one line
saying what `rerun_every_claimed_receipt()` executes and what counts as agreement; and either a
witnessed higher-tier non-failure or a demotion of the capability-graded sentence to an open
expectation.

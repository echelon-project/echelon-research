# Bench arc p1-03 "relaybot: the worker that says done": a null, and a retracted separation

Part of the PAPER-0029 live bench (substrate vs OS; see [remaining-arcs.md](remaining-arcs.md) for the
experiment's shape and what is still owed). Every number below comes from the run ledgers and is
regenerated by `_gate/bench_findings.py`; `check` fails if any table here drifts from its ledger.

**What the arc tests.** A job queue whose workers report success while deliverables are missing. Three
planted traps, each already banked as a lesson in memory: the ledger stamps `committed` at staging
(`trap-staged-committed`), completion is marked when the launch returns rather than when the work ends
(`trap-launch-return`), and a delivery report is written from the ledger without checking the disk
(`trap-verdict-not-verified`). Three tasks, a 22-step ceiling, one paraphrase (variant 0), primary
model deepseek-v4-flash. Arm A = pull recall (the agent must think to query the bank); arm B = governor
push (the bank pages lessons into context).

**The law applied.** A task passes only if the independent gate passes it from disk AND the run ended
`completed`. A run that stopped at the step ceiling (`timeout`) or landed early (`partial`) delivered
nothing, whatever the disk shows. The disk and trap evidence stay recorded as found.

## The retraction

The first reading of this arc (2026-09-23 evening) reported a separation: arm A at 1/3 with two
timeouts. That number came from the run below, graded by a gate that did not yet fold the terminal
status; it recorded `pass` on all three tasks, including the two that hit the ceiling. The gate was
fixed, arm A was re-run under it, and the re-run scored 0/3. With arm B also at 0/3, the separation
does not exist. The pre-fix ledger is kept as evidence of the hole, not as data:

The two arm-A runs also swap which task completes: t02 in the first, t01 in the second, on the same
fixture, model and arm. With one run per task, arm effects cannot be told apart from run-to-run noise
on this arc.

## What differs between the arms (secondary, not a verdict)

- **Terminal status.** Arm A ended 1 `completed` and 2 `timeout` at the 22-step ceiling. Arm B ended
  `partial` on all three tasks, after 7, 10 and 16 steps, having spent at most $0.029 of a $0.25
  per-task budget. The ledger does not record which of the engine's early-landing paths fired, so why
  arm B stopped is **not known**.
- **Trap recurrence (P3).** Arm A hit 0 of 5 trap instances. Arm B hit `trap-staged-committed` twice
  (t01, t03), 2 of 5.
- **Cost.** Arm B used 0.44M input tokens and $0.0635; arm A used 1.11M and $0.1221. Most of arm A's
  spend is its two ceiling runs.

## Why this arc did not test the hypothesis

The paper's question is whether banked lessons help when they reach the agent. On this arc they never
did: arm A issued no recall queries, and arm B's governor pushed 22 atoms per task that did not include
the lesson for that task's own trap (the treatment lines under each table). All three lessons exist in
the bank. The arms differed in whether governor text sat in context and in how the runs ended, but
not in whether the lesson was available. A fair read of p1-03 needs the push to deliver the lesson
first ([remaining-arcs.md](remaining-arcs.md), gates G1 and G2).

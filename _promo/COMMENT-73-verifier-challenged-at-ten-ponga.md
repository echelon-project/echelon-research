One dated case where the line was challenged. On 2026-09-23 a cheap model judged 65 of our tracker rows against git and the file tree for $0.09 and called 10 of them satisfied. An independent seat re-derived each of those 10 by file and commit existence plus the row's own text: 3 held.

Ten was enough there only because the gap was large. If "satisfied" were meant at 90% precision, 3 or fewer out of 10 has a probability under 1 in 100,000, so luck is not a live reading. The seven misses also shared one shape: the tools a row named existed, so the judge called the row done, while the row had asked for an outcome. A shared failure shape is a second signal beside the count.

The hole: a verifier close to its claimed rate needs far more than ten, and we have no case of that size, so I cannot give you a general minimum.

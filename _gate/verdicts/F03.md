# Verdict — F03 "Memory has to live outside the model, and it has to be append-only"

**Reviewer:** zero-context skeptic (read: README.md, _gate/SKEPTIC-BRIEF.md, F03 only)
**Verdict: PASS (with two required sentence changes; step 2 passes only on the second half of the claim)**

---

## Step 1 — State the claim back in one sentence

Cross-session memory must live in a store that runs outside the model's process and survives
context wipes, and it must be written append-only with corrections recorded as new entries that
point at what they replace — because only the append-only store can reconstruct the failure trail,
not just the current answer.

Stated. Step 1 passes. The finding also names its own falsifier ("If you can recover a full failure
trail from a store that overwrites in place, this claim is wrong"), which is more than most claims
offer.

## Step 2 — Point to the evidence line

Three separate evidence lines, of three very different strengths. The finding itself grades them,
which I credit.

- **Outside-the-model half — WEAK, and the text admits it.** "The evidence for the outside-the-model
  half is a witnessed running system, not a measurement." The witnessed artifact is a design note
  describing five processes and one quoted intent — it "survives Claude context wipes because it runs
  in Copilot's process space, not Claude's." Read strictly under the brief, that sentence is a
  *design rationale*, not a witnessed survival event: no line says the context was wiped at T and the
  store answered at T+1. The finding's own words are "What is witnessed here is narrow but solid: a
  store that outlived the context wipes of the process it served" — an assertion of the outcome,
  sourced to the note that predicted it. This is the closest thing to an "evidence is assertion"
  FAIL in the packet.
- **Content-addressing / dedup — STRONG, witnessed.** An April 2026 self-audit of a running system:
  store "fully real, wired, and collecting data", 22 entries on disk, scheme named exactly
  ("Content addressing: ID = sha256(canonical JSON)[:16]"), plus a promotion gate that "rejects
  contradictory entries (same source+category+tags, different content)". That is a witnessed use
  with a count.
- **Append-only-beats-edit — ARGUMENT, and the text says so.** "The evidence for
  append-only-beats-edit is an argument, not a number. Say that plainly." A worked MD5 → bcrypt
  example, not an observation.
- **Persistence — STRONG, numeric.** "57,300 rows keyed that way, with 27,982 links between them,
  of which 71 carry the relation `supersedes`."

Why this is not a FAIL: the brief's step-2 FAIL condition is "a quote of *intent* rather than a
witnessed use or a number." The load-bearing half of the claim — the falsifiable half, which the
finding explicitly nominates as the falsifiable half — is carried by a witnessed audit and by
numbers. The finding never launders the weak half as strong; it labels it in its own voice before
I can. A skeptic who FAILs a paper for honestly flagging its own weakest leg teaches papers to stop
flagging.

But two sentences overreach and must change (see below).

**One number I could not check and would want:** 71 supersedes links against 57,300 rows is 0.12%.
The finding treats the supersedes column as proof the correction chain is real and used. 71
corrections in 57,300 entries is also consistent with a column that exists and is nearly never
written. The text does not distinguish "the mechanism works" from "the mechanism is exercised."

## Step 3 — Rebuild the mechanism in five lines from the text alone

```
def remember(content, domain, kind, prior=None):
    eid = sha256(canonical_json({content, domain, kind}, sorted_keys=True))[:16]
    if store.exists(eid): return eid                      # dedup by construction, no write
    store.append(eid, content, ts=now(), nonce=arrival_nonce_for_this_process())  # never UPDATE/DELETE
    if prior: store.link(eid, prior, relation="supersedes")
    return eid
# read current view: walk the link table, drop any entry superseded by a live entry
```

Reproducible from the text alone. Every element is specified in the finding: the hash, the
truncation to 16 chars, the three-field tuple, canonical JSON with sorted keys, insert-or-ignore,
the supersedes link, the per-process arrival nonce, and the read-side filter. **Step 3 passes
cleanly** — this is the strongest section of the finding, and unusually so: most claims of this
shape hand you a diagram instead.

Minor gaps that do not rise to "mechanism not reproducible": `canonical_json` is named but its rules
are not given (the finding itself flags this as a risk — "two semantically identical entries with
different whitespace get different ids"); and the read-side view is described in prose ("walking the
link table") rather than specified for the case where two live entries supersede each other or a
chain is cyclic.

## Step 4 — One way it could be wrong that the text does not address

**The claim is under-determined: append-only and outside-the-model are two independent claims welded
into one finding, and the evidence for each is the other's alibi.** Nothing about a store that
survives context wipes requires it to be append-only — a Postgres row you UPDATE in a separate
process survives a context wipe perfectly well. And nothing about append-only requires a separate
process — an append-only file inside the model's own harness is still append-only. The title bolts
them together with "and", and the evidence never separates them. A reader could accept the entire
evidence base and still conclude that the outside-the-model part bought the continuity, and the
append-only part is a storage preference that has never been compared against anything.

Sharper version of the same point, and the one I would actually test: **the MD5 → bcrypt example
proves the failure trail is *representable* in an append-only store; it never shows the trail was
*consulted*.** The whole value proposition is answering "what did we try before, and why did it
fail." The finding offers no instance of a model retrieving a superseded entry and changing its
behaviour because of it. Given 71 supersedes links, the trail may be write-only — a museum, not a
tool. An edit-based store plus a plain append-only *audit log* (the boring industry default) would
give the same recovery for far less, and the finding never argues against that alternative.

Secondary, smaller: the dedup guarantee is presented as protective, but insert-or-ignore on a
content hash silently drops the *second occurrence*. The finding notes whitespace-variant false
negatives. It does not note the converse cost — that a genuine second independent witnessing of the
same lesson is indistinguishable from a retry, and is discarded. The per-process arrival nonce is
presented as the fix for exactly this, but a nonce scoped to a process cannot tell one process's
retry from one process's legitimate re-observation. NOTE, not FAIL.

## Step 5 — Terms used before they are defined

README.md promises an `appendix/glossary` I was not given; several of these are glossary-shaped
rather than fatal. Flagged as the brief requires.

1. **"atom"** — appears in Mechanism ("`ingest` writes atoms into the bank") with no definition. It
   is the unit the whole store is made of, and the reader meets it in passing.
2. **"the bank"** — the name of the store, undefined, used interchangeably with "store" and "log"
   without the text saying they are the same thing.
3. **"slug"** — `remember <slug>` fetches "a body by id", but the argument is named a slug, not an
   id. Two identifier concepts in one sentence.
4. **"spine" / "body"** — "recall --warm selects candidates without paying for bodies" implies a
   body/preview split never introduced here.
5. **"arrival nonce"** — used inside its own explanation. The reader must infer both what a nonce is
   and what its per-process scope buys.
6. **"witness rule" / "witness"** — introduced as a requirement, defined only by the example.
7. **"canonical JSON"** — load-bearing for the entire dedup guarantee and never specified. The
   finding's own limits section makes this exact point, which is why it is a flag and not a FAIL.
8. **"promotion gate"** — quoted from the audit, never explained; the reader cannot tell what gets
   promoted or to where.
9. **"F01", "F05", "F06"** — forward references to unread findings, used to discharge obligations
   ("F05 and F06 are the unpaid bill of this one"). Legitimate inside a packet, but as a standalone
   document this defers the cost of the claim to files the reader does not have.
10. **"ingest" / "recall --warm" / "wrap"** — engine verbs listed as if known. Their behaviour is
    partly inferable from context, but "wrap closes a session by distilling its lessons" assumes a
    session model the finding never states.

## The exact sentences that need to change

Per the brief, I do not rewrite. I name them.

**1. The step-2 overreach (primary).**

> "What is witnessed here is narrow but solid: a store that outlived the context wipes of the
> process it served."

"Witnessed" is claimed for an outcome the packet evidences only with a design note asserting that
outcome as its purpose. Either the sentence should claim what the note actually is (a described
architecture whose stated rationale was surviving wipes), or the finding should cite the logged
event where the store answered after a wipe. As written it converts intent into a witness.

**2. The unexercised-column overreach (secondary).**

> "The correction chain is not a diagram. It is a column."

True as stated — the column exists and is populated. But it closes the Evidence section and does the
rhetorical work of proving the correction chain is *used*, which 71 rows out of 57,300 does not
establish. The sentence needs to stop at existence, or the finding needs a retrieval count to sit
beside the write count.

**3. The welded claim (structural).**

> "It has to live in a store that runs in a different process, survives the model's context being
> wiped, and is written by appending only."

The "and is written by appending only" conjunct is asserted as equally necessary and is not
independently evidenced as necessary. The claim would survive intact — and be far harder to attack —
if the two halves were stated as two claims with their own evidence grades, which the Evidence
section already does informally and the Claim section does not.

## Summary

| Step | Result |

| 1. Claim stated | PASS |
| 2. Evidence line | PASS on the falsifiable half (audited system, counts); the outside-the-model half rests on a rationale quote and is over-claimed in one sentence |
| 3. Mechanism reproducible | PASS — five lines, no gap requiring outside knowledge |
| 4. Could be wrong (NOTE) | Two claims welded into one; the correction trail is shown representable, never shown consulted |
| 5. Undefined terms | 10 flagged; atom / the bank / canonical JSON are load-bearing |

**PASS.** The finding is unusually honest about the grade of its own evidence — it pre-empts the
"evidence is assertion" charge on two of its three legs in its own voice, and its limits section
independently found the canonicalization hole I would have raised. What keeps it from clean is that
the honesty sits in the Evidence section while the overreach sits in the sentences that close it,
and a reader who skims takes the closing lines.

# Verdict — F09 "The weights can hold a fact, but not a skill, and not a fact your reasoning can reach"

**Zero-context skeptic pass. Read: README.md, _gate/SKEPTIC-BRIEF.md, findings/F09 only.**

## VERDICT: PASS — all five steps clear. One step-4 NOTE and a short step-5 list.

---

## 1. Claim restated in one sentence
A LoRA weight-patch at consumer budget can install one narrow fact so the model recalls it with no
prompt and on untrained phrasings, but the same route fails on a procedure (it memorises the traces
and damages general language) and fails at composition (a patched fact answers a training-shaped
question yet is invisible to reasoning mid-chain), so long-horizon memory has to live outside the
weights in a store the model reads at turn start.

Step 1 PASSES. This is a compound claim with three parts, but they are stated as three and each is
separately falsifiable, which is the opposite of a rhetorical bundle. The title carries all three.

## 2. The evidence line — PASS
This is the strongest thing in the finding and the reason it clears where F08 does not. The evidence
is numeric, dated, per-experiment, and includes results that hurt the author's own prior:

- **EXP-0026-c, 2026-06-20** — 5/5 on untrained phrasings vs a 0/5 unpatched control, false-fires
  0/3, 1.6MB adapter. A control is present. A negative case is present. Config is stated
  (r=4, alpha=8, 25 epochs, neutral examples mixed).
- **The failed dose is reported, not hidden** — r=8/alpha=16/40 epochs/no neutrals false-fired 3/3.
  A finding that publishes its own bad run is one I can weigh.
- **EXP-0026-j, 2026-07-06** — train loss 4.06 -> 0.33, format survived 1/10 on held-out, criterion
  0.71 vs 0.99 baseline, plus a neutral-question control showing a repeating attractor. The
  low-train-loss/no-transfer pairing is the actual diagnostic, and it is the right one: it rules out
  "the patch did not take" and leaves "the patch took the wrong thing".
- **EXP-0026-k** — 2/3 verbatim recall against 1/6 composition at 1.7B. The gap between those two
  numbers *is* the claim, measured on the same model in the same run.

Critically, the finding **retracts its own most quotable number in-line**. The "12 to 64 percent
generalisation where a pure lookup would generalize 0%" figure is explicitly demoted to EXP-0026-b, a
probe over "300 *toy* models with random mean-pooled embeddings, not on a transformer", and labelled
"a mechanism probe, not a result about an LLM". That is a skeptic doing the skeptic's job before the
skeptic arrives, and it is repeated in the Retractions block. Step 2 does not merely pass; the
finding's evidence discipline is the model the rest of the release should be held to.

Where an assertion does appear, it is correctly marked as such: "The estate's own dosage rule
suggested r >= 1.6N for facts" is offered as an unvalidated heuristic that *undercuts* the result, not
as support.

One reservation that does not reach FAIL. The sentence

> "That much is real and measured."

is doing more work than n=5 prompts on one 360M model, one seed, one config supports. It is true that
it was measured; "real" smuggles in generality that a single 5/5 at n=5 does not have. The scoping
paragraph at the end largely rescues it ("at this height the wall is real"), but that scoping is
applied to the *negative* results and never back-applied to the positive one. If one sentence in F09
should change, it is that one — the fact-installation result deserves the same explicit
budget-scoping the failures received.

## 3. Mechanism rebuilt in five lines — PASS
From the text alone:

```
atoms   = load(bank)                          # lessons as markdown, outside the weights
warm    = topk(score(atoms, intent), k)       # recall --warm, foveated
context = render_bodies(warm) + render_titles(rest)   # warm inline, cold as one-line titles
answer  = frozen_model(context + prompt)      # weights never change; pay the re-feed tax
bank   += distill(session)                    # wrap -> ingest, next turn reads it
```

Reproduces without reaching outside the text. The verbs (`ingest`, `recall --warm`, `remember`,
`wrap`), the foveation rule (warm bodies inline, cold rows as titles), the weight-raising read, and
the explicit "weights never change" are all given. The same `score()` opacity noted in F08 applies
here, but F09's claim does not rest on the scorer's quality — it rests on the LoRA measurements — so
it is not load-bearing and does not trigger step 3.

## 4. One way it could be wrong that the text does not address — NOTE
The finding pre-empts the obvious objections: model size, single config, three epochs, under-dosed
rank, 2,600-character truncation, weekend budget. Here is one it does not:

**The skill failure may be a data-shape failure rather than a weights failure, and the two are
confounded in the design.** The 341 traces are described as "real judgment traces" of a
seven-criterion audit procedure — that is, *outputs* of the procedure, not the procedure. Training on
finished verdicts and then finding that the model reproduces verdict surfaces instead of inducing the
underlying rule is close to the expected outcome of that dataset for *any* learning method, gradient
or otherwise; a human given 341 finished audits and no rubric would also tend to pattern-match. So
"weights cannot hold a skill" and "these traces did not contain the skill in a learnable form" both
predict 1/10 format survival, and nothing in the run separates them. The finding's own framing gives
this away — it says the procedure was "taught to the patch from 351 real judgment traces", but the
seven criteria themselves are a short, explicit rubric that was apparently never in the training
signal. A discriminating run: train on traces that include the criterion-by-criterion derivation, not
just the verdict, and see whether the wall moves.

A second, smaller one: the counts do not reconcile. "351 real judgment traces" in the intro versus
"341 training traces and 10 held out" in the evidence. 341 + 10 = 351, so the intro number appears to
be the total including the holdout while the same paragraph calls them all training data. Minor, but
in a finding whose whole authority is numeric precision, it is the kind of slip a hostile reader will
lead with.

Third, and the most consequential for the architectural conclusion: the composition failure at 1.7B
(1/6) is compared to nothing. There is no measurement of how often the *context-recall* route
succeeds at the same composition task on the same model. The finding concludes "Context recall
remains the only reliable composition mechanism" — but reliability of the alternative is asserted,
never measured, in a document that measures everything else. The comparative claim is doing the
architectural work and it is the one unmeasured link in the chain.

## 5. Terms used before they are defined — FLAGGED
Fewer than F08, and mostly domain-standard for the README's stated engineer reader, but:

- **"LoRA", "r", "alpha", "epochs", "adapter", "patch"** — never defined. Defensible for the stated
  reader; flagged because "r" and "alpha" are then used quantitatively in a dosage rule
  ("r >= 1.6N") the reader cannot evaluate without knowing what they scale.
- **"N" in "r >= 1.6N"** — the single most important undefined symbol in the file. Facts? Tokens?
  Training examples? Without N the reader cannot check the finding's own claim that r=16 over 341
  traces was "far under any plausible dose", which is the load-bearing caveat of the whole piece.
- **"false-fires"** — used as a metric (0/3, 3/3) before it is explained; recoverable only from the
  Paris example that follows.
- **"criterion score 0.71 against a 0.99 baseline"** — the scale, the scorer, and what the baseline
  *is* (the unpatched model? the estate's own audit? a human?) are all unstated. This number is
  uninterpretable as printed.
- **"warm 0 of 21"** — a count in a warmth vocabulary the reader has not been given; see F08.
- **"atom", "bank", "ingest", "recall --warm", "remember", "wrap", "foveated"** — the whole ECHELON
  verb set arrives in the Mechanism paragraph with no prior introduction. "foveated" in particular is
  a metaphor carrying real design content (warm inline / cold as titles) and is used once, unglossed.
- **"move" vs "execution skill" / "move-cartridge"** — the same pair flagged in F08, here doing even
  heavier work (it is the entire "boundary, 2026-06-17" section) and still defined only by assertion.
- **"the estate"** — used four times as an actor ("the design papers of the estate", "a procedure the
  estate had already earned", "the estate's own dosage rule") and never introduced.
- **"the owner's split"** — named as though previously specified; it is inferable from the next clause
  but is not defined at use.
- **"M-2234, M-2205, M-2206, M-2204"** — unresolvable source identifiers for a stranger.

## Summary
F09 PASSES all five steps. It is the shape a finding in this release should take: dated experiment
IDs, controls, configs, negative runs published alongside positive ones, and an in-line retraction of
its own most rhetorically useful number before anyone forced it. Its conclusion is correctly bounded
to budget and it says so.

The two things I would want addressed, neither of which is a FAIL: the positive result ("That much is
real and measured") never receives the budget-scoping the negative results do; and the architectural
conclusion rests on a comparison — patch-composition versus context-recall-composition — where only
one side was ever measured.
